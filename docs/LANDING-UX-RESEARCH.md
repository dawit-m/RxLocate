# RxLocate landing-page direction

## Chosen direction

The landing page now follows a **quiet-luxury, search-first** direction. The visual language uses warm mineral off-white, graphite type, a single muted teal action color, hairline borders, soft depth, generous whitespace, and a restrained serif accent for moments of warmth. Luxury comes from proportion, pacing, and clarity rather than heavy color or decorative effects.

The copy has been intentionally reduced. The hero makes one promise and presents one primary task. Supporting sections use short labels, compact proof points, and one sentence of context. Detailed information belongs in the patient finder, pharmacy workspace, documentation, or support surfaces rather than on the homepage.

## Information architecture

The landing page is organized into five quiet chapters. The hero answers what RxLocate does and lets someone search immediately. The availability card demonstrates what a useful result looks like. The three-step proof strip explains the service in seconds. The story section builds trust around context, honesty, and the two-sided pharmacy relationship. The final dark call to action is reserved for pharmacy teams and does not compete with the patient search task.

## Research references

The direction was informed by Apple Health’s short benefit-led positioning and explicit privacy/science proof, Stripe’s restrained conversion hierarchy and product evidence, Headspace’s calm audience pathways, and the search-first patterns found in Notion, Vercel, Algolia, and modern healthcare discovery products.

## Content rules

The page avoids hype, urgency, unsupported guarantees, diagnosis language, and long feature lists. Status and availability are described with precise operational language: pharmacies publish listings, stock can change, and patients should call ahead. The product is positioned as a decision layer for discovery, not as a replacement for a pharmacist or clinician.
